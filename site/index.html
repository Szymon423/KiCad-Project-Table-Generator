How it works

From a few words to the silkscreen

No image editor and no conversion step. The table goes from the app to the board through the clipboard.

01

Type the rows

Add the project name, the author, the date, the revision. Drop in a logo. The preview redraws as you type, and the corner shows the finished size in millimetres.

02

Copy, then paste in KiCad

Press Ctrl+K in the app and Ctrl+V in the PCB editor. The table arrives as a footprint on the front silkscreen, already at the right size. Flip it in KiCad to put it on the back.

03

It is on the board

What you saw in the preview is what gets printed, logo included. Save the table as a project file, and next time you only change the revision and copy again.

What it does

The data sheet

Everything the app can do, in the format engineers read first.

Sizing
Letter height, line width and padding in millimetres. Or fix the height of the whole table and let everything scale to fit.
Edges
No margin around the table. Its outer lines are the edges, so the size you see is the size you get.
Logo
PNG, JPG or SVG; chosen, dropped or pasted. Turned in 90° steps or inverted on its own. Empty edges of the file are trimmed, so one value sets the space around it.
Text
Any number of rows, left or centred. DejaVu Sans and Liberation Sans are built in, and you can load your own font file.
To KiCad
Copied as a footprint for pasting, or saved as a .kicad_mod file. Normal or inverted (white on black).
Other output
PNG at the resolution you choose, with the DPI written into the file, and SVG.
Projects
Saved as one file with the logo and font inside. The app also remembers your last table by itself.
Privacy
Everything happens in your browser. Your logo and text are not uploaded anywhere.
Runs on
Any modern browser, with nothing to install. It can also be built as a desktop app for Linux.
How it was made

Written by an AI, directed by a person

This app is vibe-coded, and this page does not hide it. The code was written by an AI assistant, Claude Code. Szymon decided what the app should do, tried every stage, and sent back what to change.

It began as a small Python script that made a PNG for KiCad's Image Converter. The rewrite keeps the idea and drops the detour: the app now traces its own picture into the polygons KiCad needs.

It is a personal tool that works for its author, not an audited product, and it has no automated tests. Bug reports are welcome on GitHub.
